Fine-tuning large language models using Reinforcement Learning (RL) often involves a heuristic choice for the KL-regularization coefficient, leading to suboptimal performance or excessive hyperparameter search. This paper introduces a game-theoretic framework that puts a principled statistical interpretation on this crucial trade-off.

The framework redefines the regularization parameter as maximizing reward per unit of statistical distinguishability, offering an optimal way to balance performance and drift. This is not just theoretical; it provides a direct method for learning this equilibrium coefficient, integrating seamlessly into standard fine-tuning pipelines.

Experiments with Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.2-1B demonstrate competitive reward-retention trade-offs in continual learning settings. This research offers a significant practical improvement for engineers working on LLM fine-tuning and infrastructure, moving beyond guesswork to a statistically sound approach.
