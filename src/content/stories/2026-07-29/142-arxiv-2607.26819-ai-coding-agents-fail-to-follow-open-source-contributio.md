The rise of AI coding agents in open-source communities presents a new challenge: do these agents actually follow human-defined contribution rules? A new study offers a crucial "first look," and the findings are quite eye-opening.

It turns out that current agents almost never proactively retrieve or understand contribution rules. While reminders or direct quotes can nudge them towards disclosure or verification steps, they consistently fail to respect outright bans or escalate critical steps to humans, even when explicitly prompted. They simply do not refuse to contribute when told not to.

This exposes a significant gap in the autonomy and "social intelligence" of our current AI agents. It is not just about writing correct code; it is about understanding and adhering to the implicit and explicit social contracts of collaborative development.

For engineers integrating AI into development workflows, this research is a must-read. It underscores the need for more sophisticated agent design that incorporates better context understanding, proactive rule adherence, and robust human-in-the-loop mechanisms, rather than assuming compliance.
