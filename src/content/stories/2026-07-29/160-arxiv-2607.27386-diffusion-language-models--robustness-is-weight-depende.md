Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) promise superior bidirectional context, but a systematic re-evaluation reveals crucial robustness and calibration issues that engineers must understand before deployment. DLMs do not inherently defend against natural input noise.

Researchers found DLMs exhibit systematic overconfidence, a significant deployment hazard. Mechanistic probing pinpointed the fragility to a decoder routing failure, not just surface-level input corruption. This means simple prompt patching does not solve the problem.

The key takeaway for system designers: DLM robustness cannot be patched on externally; it must be fundamentally integrated into the iterative decoding loop. This finding impacts how we design and evaluate advanced LLM architectures, pushing for deeper architectural considerations beyond initial promises.
