LLMs are increasingly used for reasoning, but how well do they handle logical inference over probability? A new benchmark reveals a concerning truth: most models perform only marginally better than random chance when dealing with probabilistic operators like "probably" or "might."

The research exposed pervasive answer biases, where models systematically prefer "Yes" or "No" independent of logical form. Out of 29 models tested, only nine exceeded random performance on these critical inference tasks, suggesting a fundamental weakness rather than superficial pattern matching.

For senior engineers building AI agents or applied AI systems that demand robust logical and probabilistic reasoning, this finding is crucial. It underscores the need for careful design of agentic pipelines and verification steps where certainty and nuanced inference are required, rather than assuming inherent competence from LLMs.

Do not assume LLMs can reason probabilistically.
