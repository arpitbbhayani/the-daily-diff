LLM serving consumes enormous energy, primarily due to data movement between limited on-chip cache and off-chip High Bandwidth Memory (HBM). As models scale, this bottleneck becomes critical for both power and thermal constraints. Addressing this requires rethinking memory architectures.

LLMET, a validated cross-layer simulation framework, provides crucial insights into how emerging monolithic 3D (M3D) integration of cache memories can revolutionize LLM serving efficiency. By expanding on-chip L2 cache from 40MB to 1GB, for example, the prefill phase of Llama3.1-70B saw a remarkable 44% reduction in chip energy. On an 8x NVIDIA B200-like platform, expanding L2 cache from 128MB to 4GB saved prefill energy by up to 24%.

These are not theoretical gains. They are concrete, quantifiable reductions directly impacting operational costs and sustainability for LLM infrastructure. The findings highlight the immense potential of ultra-large on-chip memories to solve critical energy bottlenecks and scale LLM serving systems efficiently.

Architects, this is the future of energy-efficient LLM deployment.
