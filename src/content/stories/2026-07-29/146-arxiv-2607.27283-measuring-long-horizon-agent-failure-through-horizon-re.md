Benchmarking LLM agents on long-horizon tasks is tricky. It is easy to just attribute failures to "more steps, more chances to fail." But what if earlier steps are actively making later steps harder?

This position paper introduces the crucial concept of the "horizon residual." This metric helps you differentiate between failures from simple error compounding and those from *trajectory-induced degradation* or *context rot*, where accumulated information or environment changes hinder subsequent decisions.

To truly understand why your agents fail in complex, multi-step scenarios, you need to compare actual full-task success against a baseline prediction built from short, individual stages. This comparison isolates the unique challenges of long-horizon problems.

Do not just observe long-horizon failures; diagnose their root cause.
