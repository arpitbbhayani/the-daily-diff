Latent communication in multi-agent LLM systems promises rich information exchange, but does it actually work as intended? This paper introduces a groundbreaking causal audit to tell us.

Instead of just looking at end-task performance, the audit uses controlled message replacements to dissect *what* information is being used by the receiver. It differentiates between the impact of message presence, example-specific content, and information contributed by other agents.

This methodology reveals surprising nuances; for instance, on some tasks, an 'other-example' message can retain significant value, indicating the receiver is not fully leveraging example-specific content. This is an essential diagnostic tool for engineering more effective and transparent multi-agent AI systems.
