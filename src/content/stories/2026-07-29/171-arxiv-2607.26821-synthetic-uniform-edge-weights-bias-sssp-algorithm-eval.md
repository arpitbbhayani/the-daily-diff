Are your algorithm benchmarks truly representative of real-world performance? This paper reveals a critical flaw in how Parallel SSSP algorithms are often evaluated.

Most research uses synthetic, uniformly distributed edge weights. However, real-world graphs have vastly different, often heavy-tailed, distributions. The shocking finding is that synthetic weights can *invert* the performance hierarchy of SSSP algorithms and alter optimal parameter configurations.

This is a stark reminder for any senior engineer: the data you test with profoundly impacts your conclusions. It challenges existing benchmarking standards and offers vital insights for rigorous algorithm design and system evaluation.
