In-context learning models often make predictions based on spurious correlations, even with robust signals present. This paper reveals that ICL can get "entangled by design," using artifacts in features over true causal signals, a critical flaw for deployment.

Surprisingly, simply increasing context size can amplify this spurious routing by up to 1.74 times. More data does not always mean better signal; sometimes, it just reinforces the wrong one. The authors even show that more expressive models can be more vulnerable.

They propose lightweight mitigations, such as environment-stratified context and S-swap augmentation, which drastically reduce spurious routing. For TabPFN, S-swap reduced spurious routing by 98.8 percent, simultaneously increasing causal sensitivity by 8.4 times. This offers actionable strategies for building more robust in-context learning systems.

Do not let your models learn the wrong lessons.
