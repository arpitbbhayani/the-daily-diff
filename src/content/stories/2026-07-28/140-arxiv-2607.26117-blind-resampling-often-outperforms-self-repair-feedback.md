A common assumption in AI code agents is that self-repair, where models get feedback on their failures, is always better. This paper delivers a surprising punch: for smaller models, simply trying again from scratch (blind resampling) is not just more effective, it is also 2.5-5.5x cheaper in token usage.

The core problem? Anchoring. When models are shown their previous failed attempt, they tend to reproduce near-identical code, rather than generating truly novel solutions. This "self-conditioning" costs performance points, especially at smaller model scales.

This finding radically simplifies agent design: sometimes, the best feedback is no feedback. If you are building AI code assistants or agentic systems, this research offers a critical, actionable insight to improve task success and significantly reduce operational costs. Do not look back; just try again.
