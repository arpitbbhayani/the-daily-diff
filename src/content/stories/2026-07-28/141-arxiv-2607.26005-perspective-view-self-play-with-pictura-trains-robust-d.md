Self-driving agent development often struggles with the "representation gap" - training on abstract, privileged data versus real-world camera views. Pictura tackles this head-on with a GPU-accelerated multi-agent simulator that renders each agent's egocentric view in real time.

This simulator achieves an astounding 500,000 agent steps per second (2 million images per second) on a single H100 GPU. This unprecedented scale enables self-play training directly from perspective images, avoiding the pitfalls of distilling privileged policies.

The result is Alberti, the first large-scale driving policy trained this way, approaching the performance of its privileged counterparts and even outperforming them in zero-shot transfers. This demonstrates a significant step forward for robust, scalable applied AI, showing that better context engineering for agents does not always mean more data, but rather more realistic data from the outset.
