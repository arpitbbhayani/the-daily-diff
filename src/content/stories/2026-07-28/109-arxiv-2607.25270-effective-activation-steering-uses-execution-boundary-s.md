If you are trying to steer LLM behavior, the source of your steering signals matters far more than you might think. This research reveals that strong signals come not just from where the desired behavior *appears*, but from where the model is *about to produce* it.

These 'execution-boundary states' are key. The paper empirically demonstrates that just changing the source activations dramatically alters steering success. Furthermore, it introduces 'tail subtraction' to remove shared semantics from boundary states, yielding cleaner and more stable steering signals.

For engineers aiming for fine-grained, reliable control over LLMs, this offers crucial insights into the model's internal representations. It is a deep dive into how LLMs 'think' about generating outputs, providing practical methods to achieve more predictable and steerable AI agents.
