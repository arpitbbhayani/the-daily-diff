Evaluating LLM reasoning often boils down to checking if the final answer is correct, but this misses a critical detail: a correct answer can still come from flawed reasoning. This "reasoning answer consistency gap" is a silent killer for trust in LLM outputs.

A new diagnostic, the Reasoning Answer Faithfulness Score (RAFS), offers a powerful, reference-free way to tackle this. RAFS checks for local credibility, whether the reasoning supports the answer, and stability under interventions. It combines step validity, reasoning-to-answer entailment, and counterfactual sensitivity to give a nuanced view.

For engineers deploying LLMs in critical reasoning roles, RAFS provides an auditable warning signal for silent failures that traditional accuracy metrics simply miss. Understanding the process of reasoning, not just the outcome, is key to building robust and trustworthy AI applications.

This shifts evaluation from "was it right?" to "was its thinking sound?".
