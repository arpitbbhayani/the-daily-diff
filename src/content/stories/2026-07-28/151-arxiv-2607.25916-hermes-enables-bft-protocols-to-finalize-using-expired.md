Tail latency is often the Achilles' heel of leader-based Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) protocols, particularly when leaders crash or become slow. Hermes, a new two-round rotating-leader protocol, introduces a game-changing concept: prefix consensus, which allows expired views to finalize, dramatically improving tail latency.

Traditional BFT protocols force a difficult trade-off with timeouts; Hermes sidesteps this by ensuring that any `n-f` votes can finalize the 'heaviest common prefix' of proposals, even if they do not perfectly match. This means even a partially completed view contributes to progress, transforming wasted cycles into usable outcomes. It reduces full proposal finalization to just two delta (24) in the timely honest leader case, a significant improvement.

This paper offers a deep dive into the mechanics of building truly resilient, high-performance distributed systems. Understanding how protocols like Hermes leverage prefix consensus and innovative dissemination techniques can directly inform the design of your next fault-tolerant, low-latency service. It is a critical read for anyone pushing the boundaries of distributed consensus.
