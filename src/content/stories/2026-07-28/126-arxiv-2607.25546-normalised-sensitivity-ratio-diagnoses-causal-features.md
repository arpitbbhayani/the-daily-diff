Deploying AI models is one challenge; understanding why they make decisions is another. This paper introduces the Normalised Sensitivity Ratio (NSR), a powerful post-hoc diagnostic to identify whether your already-trained model relies on causal features or spurious correlations.

Traditional methods often require access to the training pipeline, but NSR works directly on deployed models. This is critical for real-world scenarios where environments might shift subtly, like multi-site clinical data or multi-batch genomics, where spurious features can mimic causal ones.

The authors provide theoretical proofs for exact identification under specific conditions and characterize failure scenarios, giving practitioners clear criteria. This helps ensure robustness and trust in applied AI systems. Imagine debugging a model without needing to retrain or dive into complex architectures – that is the power of NSR.
