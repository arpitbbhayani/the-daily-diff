The promise of recursive self-improvement for AI agents is immense, but how do we measure an agent's "research capability" in refining its own training data? Existing benchmarks often entangle research decisions with implementation.

RSIBench-Data offers a controlled, data-centric benchmark for LLM agents acting as researchers. It tasks agents with iteratively revising training-data strategies for a fixed target model, with transparent access to training, serving, and evaluation services.

The findings are fascinating and a touch sobering: agents can improve upon initial attempts by refining strategies from feedback in over half the settings. However, this improvement is often inconsistent; later revisions frequently fail to surpass peak performance found earlier.

Trajectory analysis reveals crucial patterns in stronger runs: accurate hypotheses, validation-grounded supervision, behavior-aligned data, and the preservation of strong checkpoints. This benchmark is a vital step toward understanding how to build truly self-improving agents.
