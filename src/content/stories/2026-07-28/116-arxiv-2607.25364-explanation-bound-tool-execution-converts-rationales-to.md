Deploying AI agents that interact with external tools brings significant safety and security challenges. We cannot blindly trust an LLM's free-form rationales for executing actions.

Explanation-Bound Tool Execution (EBTE) offers a critical solution. It acts as a mediation layer, converting decision-relevant rationale content into typed action claims. These claims are then rigorously checked against server-held facts regarding intent, policy, payload, tool, risk, and provenance. This shifts the trust boundary away from the LLM's internal monologue to an auditable, server-verified process.

The formalization and extensive evaluation demonstrate its robustness: it matches all specified dispositions across 136 conformance scenarios and successfully denies 96 designated hard contradictions. This approach does not widen baseline authority; instead, it enforces it, allowing conflicts to be denied and uncertain claims reviewed, while ensuring only matching claims proceed.

This is a breakthrough for production-ready AI agents, providing a blueprint for building agentic systems with built-in safety, authorization, and auditability mechanisms that engineers can actually implement and rely on.
