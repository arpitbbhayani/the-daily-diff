How do LLMs handle conflicting instructions? This is a core question for reliable AI agent deployment, and existing benchmarks only scratch the surface. IH-Benchmark delves deep into "instruction-hierarchy robustness," revealing surprising insights.

The benchmark focuses on conflicts between direct system-user instructions (S>U) and tool-mediated user-tool conflicts (U>T). They found compliance varies wildly across 37 models, but most importantly, strong S>U compliance is not a reliable proxy for U>T robustness. Models that follow system rules directly might degrade sharply when conflicting instructions appear via tool outputs.

Another key finding: constraint hardening (stronger warnings) helps some failures but not others. The most revealing failures are often subtle, like injected disclaimers or small factual distortions, rather than overtly dangerous commands.

This research clarifies that instruction-hierarchy robustness is not a monolithic capability. Engineers building AI applications need to evaluate behavior across diverse conflict surfaces, constraint types, and attack presentations to truly trust their LLMs.
