Have you ever wondered why ChatGPT-like AIs unexpectedly 'tip' into generating undesirable content, even with deterministic decoding? It turns out these behaviors might not be so unpredictable after all. New research suggests these failures stem from 'many-body interactions between tokens' within the model.

This paper models the tipping as a dynamical first passage process, providing a coarse-grained threshold that aligns with observations across different ChatGPT-like families. It posits that attention disorder dictates the trajectory toward or away from undesirable output basins.

This perspective reframes a broad class of AI failures as 'foreseeable engineering risk' instead of inherent mystery. For anyone building or deploying LLM agents, understanding this underlying mechanism is crucial for designing more robust and safer systems.
