Building reliable AI agents in sensitive domains like healthcare requires more than just knowledge. A new benchmark, PatientAgentBench, exposes critical safety and performance gaps in patient-facing health AI agents.

This framework moves beyond static Q&A by evaluating agents in sustained, tool-using conversations with simulated patients. It uses an LLM-as-a-Jury approach, validated against licensed clinicians, scoring agents across six dimensions and over a hundred criteria.

The findings are stark: even frontier models fail on 1-3 percent of cases, often due to unverified tool outputs or omitted crisis resources. Triage quality and clinical safety are the most discriminating factors, with significant improvements needed. This confirms that static benchmarks are insufficient for evaluating autonomous agentic systems.

If you are designing, building, or deploying AI agents, this paper offers invaluable lessons on real-world challenges and rigorous evaluation strategies to ensure safety and effectiveness.
