The pursuit of full AI automation often assumes human involvement is merely a temporary stopgap until AI improves. This paper challenges that, offering a robust theory for *persistent* human participation in AI systems.

It outlines three compelling reasons: technical/complementarity (humans bring unique capabilities), normative/developmental (participation itself is valuable for agency or learning), and most importantly, emergence grounds. This last point highlights that for many complex activities, the target itself is not fully specified upfront, but emerges through interaction.

For senior engineers designing AI agents and human-in-the-loop systems, this framework is invaluable. It shifts the perspective from simply replacing humans to understanding and architecting for effective human-AI co-construction, particularly for tasks where objectives evolve. This changes how you think about agentic design.
