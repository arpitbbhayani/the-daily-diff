Being told to "ask an LLM" for an answer when facing a truly hard problem can be incredibly frustrating for senior engineers. LLMs are powerful, but they often fall short when the question requires nuance, conflicting evidence resolution, or judgment honed by years of real-world scar tissue.

There is a distinct difference between searching for information and seeking genuine expertise. When five studies conflict, a human with thirty years of experience can weigh context, understand implicit biases, and offer a trusted perspective that a language model cannot replicate.

This highlights a crucial aspect of effective engineering: knowing when to leverage AI for data synthesis and when to tap into invaluable human wisdom and shared context. Relying solely on LLMs can lead to circular reasoning or shallow insights.

Human expertise remains irreplaceable for complex, non-consensus problems.
