Before C dominated system programming, there was BLISS. Developed at Carnegie Mellon around 1970, BLISS was a typeless, block-structured language pivotal for implementing system software. Its original compiler was renowned for extensive optimizations, forming the basis of 'The Design of an Optimizing Compiler'. Understanding BLISS offers a crucial historical perspective on system design principles and compiler engineering that laid the groundwork for today's complex software architectures. It is a deep dive into foundational engineering practices.
