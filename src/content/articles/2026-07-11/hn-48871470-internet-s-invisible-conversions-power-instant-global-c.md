The internet is an astonishing feat of distributed system design, and this article breaks it down from its core principles. Imagine sending a message across continents and receiving a reply in milliseconds; it involves radio waves, copper, fiber optics, and equipment from dozens of independent companies.

No single entity controls this global network; there is no central computer directing traffic. Each component simply hands data off to the next closest route, making it incredibly resilient and scalable.

This decentralized architecture is why the internet works reliably billions of times per second, often unnoticed until a call stutters. It is a masterclass in implicit coordination and robust design under uncertainty.

Grasping these fundamentals strengthens your ability to architect any large-scale distributed system.
