Back in 1981, Richard Stallman laid out the revolutionary vision for Emacs, an editor designed from the ground up for extreme extensibility and customization. What made it powerful was the ability for users to modify or add commands in the very language Emacs was built in.

This seminal paper teaches us that user empowerment through code-level extensibility is not just a feature; it is a fundamental design philosophy. It is not about providing a fixed set of functions, but a platform for continuous evolution by its users.

Even today, these principles resonate deeply in modern software architecture and engineering practices, reminding us to build tools that are not just used, but truly owned and shaped by their users.

Embrace true extensibility in your designs.
