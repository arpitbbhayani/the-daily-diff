Building effective multi-agent systems requires robust learning algorithms. This paper introduces the first provably efficient decentralized learning algorithms for "assistance games," where an informed human agent guides an uninformed AI assistant.

These algorithms address the challenge of optimizing a common reward function over repeated interactions, even with asymmetric information. They achieve impressive regret rates, critical for understanding the theoretical limits and practical potential of such cooperative AI setups.

For engineers working on AI agents, this research provides a strong theoretical framework for designing more effective and provably optimal interactive learning systems. It is about laying the groundwork for more intelligent and collaborative AI.
