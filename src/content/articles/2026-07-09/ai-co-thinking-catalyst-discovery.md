Frontier language models are moving beyond text generation into scientific discovery. This paper unveils a human-AI co-thinking framework that uses LLMs to reason over complex chemical reaction networks.

By strictly constraining models to explicit reaction networks, the system identifies physical levers governing pathway competition, leading to novel catalyst-selectivity hypotheses. This led to the prospective synthesis of a copper-iron oxide catalyst with a threefold increase in acetate selectivity.

This mechanism-guided reasoning shifts the paradigm from retrospective prediction to forward-looking hypothesis generation. For engineers interested in applied AI, it showcases the immense potential of LLM reasoning for accelerating research in complex scientific domains.
