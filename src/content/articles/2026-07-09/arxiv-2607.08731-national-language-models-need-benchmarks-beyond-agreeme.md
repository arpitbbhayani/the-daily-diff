You trust an LLM to annotate your data, but are its "correct" answers based on true understanding or just surface-level correlations?

A recent study on Portugal's AMALIA, a 9B-parameter LLM, found that despite high agreement with human annotators, the model often used "correlated shortcuts" rather than deeply understanding the theoretical constructs it was supposed to infer.

This paper introduces the "recovery gap" to test if an LLM truly follows the construct's theory or just mimics outputs. This is a critical distinction when deploying LLMs for high-stakes data annotation.

Do not rely solely on agreement metrics; probe an LLM's evidential route for true validity.
