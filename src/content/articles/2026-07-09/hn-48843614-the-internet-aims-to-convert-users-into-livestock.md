The internet has fundamentally shifted from seeking readers to farming "livestock". We are now "leads" to be converted, not audiences to be informed.

This piece highlights the relentless barrage of cookie banners, newsletter pop-ups, and complex, unnecessary tools that clutter online experiences. The focus has moved entirely to optimizing funnels and capturing data, often at the expense of genuine content consumption.

As engineers, it is crucial to recognize how these design choices impact user perception and trust. Are we building systems that truly serve users, or are we inadvertently contributing to an environment where every interaction is a monetization opportunity?

We must consider the ethical implications of design and how we can foster healthier online environments.
