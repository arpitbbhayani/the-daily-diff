Ever found your software system struggling with "same capacity, less throughput"? This often mirrors the "chosen scarcity" phenomenon seen in urban planning.

The article draws a brilliant parallel between housing bottlenecks caused by accumulated, individually sensible regulations and the technical debt in legacy codebases. Each "valve" added over time, though well-intentioned, can collectively cripple a system's overall efficiency.

Using real data from Austin and San Francisco, it shows an 8x difference in housing permits per capita, directly attributable to regulatory overhead, not underlying capacity. This is a stark reminder of how incremental decisions can lead to profound systemic slowdowns.

Understanding these dynamics helps us better recognize and manage the cumulative impact of architectural choices and operational processes in our own software projects.

Throughput is not just about raw resources; it is about reducing friction.
