The rise of AI in complex fields like mathematics brings a critical question: what happens if our ability to understand AI's output diminishes? This paper argues that relying on AI for research-level math while weakening human capacity to verify it is a profound strategic error. It is a wake-up call for engineering.

The author points out that mathematical capacity is not a mere byproduct of theorem production; it is infrastructure built over generations. If we cannot audit or truly interpret AI's consequential reasoning, we risk becoming dependent on systems we do not fully comprehend.

A key proposal is requiring AI systems performing critical reasoning to expose their decision-critical claims in formal, machine-checkable formats. This would shift AI reasoning from opaque persuasion to auditable structure, a paradigm shift that could significantly enhance trust and reliability in AI-driven decisions.

Consider the long-term implications for all AI-driven engineering.
