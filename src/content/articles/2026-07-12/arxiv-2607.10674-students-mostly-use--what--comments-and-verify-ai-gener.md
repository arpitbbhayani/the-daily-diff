As AI code generation tools like Copilot become ubiquitous, the way we write comments is evolving. Are you still commenting for humans, or are you effectively 'prompting' the AI?

This paper offers a fascinating multi-year analysis of student code-generation specifications. It introduces a taxonomy for comment types, revealing that students primarily write 'What' comments, focusing on desired behavior rather than 'How' to implement it.

A key finding: students spend more time verifying generated code than repeatedly rewriting comments. This suggests a significant shift in engineering practices from direct code writing to specification and validation.

Understanding these patterns helps us adapt our own workflows and teaches us how to write more effective specifications for AI tools. It is about becoming a better 'AI conductor' rather than just a 'code writer.'

The future of coding is about intelligent collaboration.
