The Dinosaur Input Device (DID) was a fascinating piece of engineering that bridged the gap between traditional stop-motion and cutting-edge CGI for Jurassic Park. This innovation saved Phil Tippett's animators from extinction, literally.

Imagine taking years of accumulated expertise in physical animation and translating it directly into digital models. The DID was a sensor-covered armature shaped like a dinosaur, allowing animators to manipulate it physically.

These physical movements were then directly translated to the computer-generated dinosaur models. This meant animators could leverage their deep understanding of dinosaur motion in the new digital realm.

The DID was not just a clever tool; it ensured invaluable artistic skill was preserved and enhanced rather than discarded. It also earned a Technical Achievement Award from the Academy.

This story shows how innovative tool design can solve profound workflow challenges and adapt human expertise to new technologies.
