Ever wondered how John Deere maintains a near-monopoly on farm equipment repair? It is all about the software and embedded systems. Modern tractors are not just mechanical beasts; they are sophisticated machines controlled by Electronic Control Units (ECUs). Deere has designed its equipment so that repairs often require specific electronic diagnostic tools, which are only fully available to authorized dealers. This creates a choke point, forcing farmers to use expensive official services. The debate around the "right-to-repair" is fundamentally about controlling access to embedded software and diagnostics. This is a critical example of how proprietary software in hardware creates significant challenges for consumers and independent businesses.
