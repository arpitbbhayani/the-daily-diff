What killed the hacker culture many of us grew up in? George Hotz argues it is spectacle, performativity, and the subtle erosion of genuine participation. He posits that streams become mere predictions of desired content, reflecting a loss of truthful self-expression.

This shift extends to our interaction with AI, where we "prompt" and feel we are steering, but the true extent of our agency becomes blurred. It is a profound observation on the illusion of control in an increasingly mediated world.

Hotz highlights how readily we consume pre-digested experiences, likening it to a caged tiger preferring "meat slop" over the hunt. This calls into question the depth of engagement and learning in an era of abundant, passive content consumption.

This article is a vital read for any engineer grappling with personal growth and effectiveness in a rapidly evolving tech landscape. It challenges us to reconsider what genuine contribution and intellectual challenge truly mean amidst the pervasive influence of AI and digital spectacle.
