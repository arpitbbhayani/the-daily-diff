Many successful companies eventually develop "competence blindness," a critical organizational flaw. This is where, like the Mexican cavefish that lost its eyes but retained its eye genes, companies forget the core competencies that initially drove their success.

During periods of rapid growth, hiring often prioritizes speed over a clear understanding of fundamental skills, creating teams that only know the "cave" - the company's current, potentially suboptimal, internal environment. These engineers then replicate this bias in future hiring.

This phenomenon explains why some once-innovative firms struggle to adapt or maintain engineering excellence, unknowingly selecting for conformity rather than true capability.

Recognizing this organizational trap is essential for maintaining robust engineering practices and fostering genuine career growth.
