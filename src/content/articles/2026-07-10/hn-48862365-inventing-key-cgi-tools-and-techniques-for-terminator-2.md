Creating the T-1000's liquid metal effects in "Terminator 2" required inventing entirely new CGI tools and techniques from scratch. This was not a simple task for ILM's surprisingly small computer graphics department back in 1991.
Engineers developed bespoke software like 'Make Sticky' and 'Body Sock' to achieve what was then unimaginable on screen. This oral history illuminates the resourcefulness and ingenuity of a team pushing the boundaries of what software could do.
It offers a powerful look into the early days of advanced software development, showcasing how fundamental engineering practices and creative problem-solving led to groundbreaking cinematic achievements.
You will see how a handful of engineers, without existing frameworks, built complex systems that changed an industry.
