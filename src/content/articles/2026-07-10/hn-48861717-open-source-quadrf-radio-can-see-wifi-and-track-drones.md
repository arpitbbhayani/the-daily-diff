Imagine an open-source phased-array radio built on a Raspberry Pi 5 that can track drones and even "see" WiFi signals through walls. That is the QuadRF, a remarkable piece of engineering that leverages FPGA for picosecond-level timing and advanced beamforming.

This project demonstrates the sheer power of modern signal processing capabilities in a compact, accessible form factor. It is not just a gadget; it represents a scalable system, with the potential to chain multiple modules for high-power radio experiments.

The project highlights how innovative hardware and software integration can achieve feats previously limited to specialized government or commercial systems. It is a testament to the open-source community's ability to push boundaries.

Engineers should take note of the elegant blend of off-the-shelf components with custom logic to solve complex RF challenges. This is practical system design at its finest, showing what is truly possible when creativity meets cutting-edge technology.
