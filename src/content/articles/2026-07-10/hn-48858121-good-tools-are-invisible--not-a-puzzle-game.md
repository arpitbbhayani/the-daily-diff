Good tools are invisible. This article makes a powerful case that a tool's primary purpose is to simplify your work, not to offer a "puzzle game" of overcoming its shortcomings.

Many engineers fall into the trap of praising tools for how much "fun" it is to work around their limitations or build complex macros for simple tasks. But if your tool demands constant problem-solving, it is hindering your productivity.

The goal is to achieve a state where your editor or system melts into the background. It should empower you to focus on the problem at hand, not on the tool itself. When a tool cannot handle something easily, it breaks that invisibility, and that friction is not "fun"-it is an impediment.

Consider your own workflow. Are you truly productive, or are you just good at solving the puzzles your tools present? True mastery comes when the tool disappears.
