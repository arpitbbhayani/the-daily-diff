Alan Kay famously called Lisp the "Maxwell's equations of software." This essay unpacks what that truly means and why Lisp holds such a revered place in computer science history.

Just as Maxwell's equations elegantly describe electromagnetism, a few lines of Lisp code reveal the essence of programming itself: the language can define and interpret itself.

The article guides you through building a modern, executable equivalent of the foundational Lisp interpreter. You do not need prior Lisp knowledge.

Understanding Lisp's simple yet powerful core concepts can fundamentally change how you think about programming, language design, and the architecture of software.
