The global AI landscape is seeing a fascinating divergence: China is advancing its soft power with open-source, open-weight AI models, while the US largely sticks to proprietary, closed-source approaches.

Andrew Ng points out that "open-weight models" are published online with all their learned numbers, allowing anyone to download and run AI on their own laptop. This fosters adaptability and wider adoption globally.

In contrast, proprietary models like those from OpenAI or Google Gemini only allow users to send prompts, keeping the underlying weights opaque. This limits external innovation and deployment flexibility.

This strategic choice by China is impacting how AI is built and utilized worldwide, potentially accelerating AI development in many regions that can freely access and modify these models. It is a different philosophy shaping the future of AI infrastructure.

Open-weight versus closed-weight: a critical divide in global AI strategy.
